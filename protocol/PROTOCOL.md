# BridgeGuard: frozen evaluation protocol

Frozen before any Zone C score was computed. Development data: the low-background simulated stream
(`baseline_data/interleaved/`). The same protocol is re-applied unchanged to the trace-driven testbed.
Everything here is regenerated by `run_dev.py` with `configs/protocol.yaml`.

## 1. Data and zones
- **Background:** `normal_interleaved.csv` only. Chronological 60/20/20 split of the calendar span into zones A/B/C, with 60-min purge gaps (`lockbox/manifest.json`, SHA-256 per zone file). The A–B purge falls inside a 4.85 h hole in the trace and removes nothing; the B–C purge removes 2,274 messages.
- **Attacks:** injected into Zone C only, as 30 bounded episodes per copy, 3 copies per attack (A2, A5-S, A5-R, A5-P, A5-M, A5-U), each episode inside one stretch, ≥ 1 h apart (`lockbox/injections.json`, injector code hash included). Zones A and B are never injected.
- **Labels:** exist only in `episodes.json` and the `labels_*.csv` files. They are read only by the final evaluation.

## 2. Windows and scoring unit
- **Windows:** 60 min, 10-min stride, same grid for all traffic; every window with ≥ 1 message is kept; features from `bridgeguard/features.py`.
- **Scoring unit, complete hours:** every 6th window of the zone's grid, anchored at the first window that contains a message, kept only if it lies entirely inside one stretch (no gap > 1 h). Grid anchored per zone, **not** restarted per stretch, so the counts are:

| Zone | Complete hours |
|---|---|
| A | 380 (323 fit + 57 held out = last 15%, chronologically) |
| B | 119 (calibration) |
| C (clean) | 133 (false-alarm test) |

  (Restarting the grid in each stretch would give 129 Zone B hours; that rule is **not** used.)

## 3. Features (label-free selection on Zone A windows; `results/dev/selected_features.json`)
Ten features: `alert_rate_acceleration`, `freq_rolling_std_5w`, `consecutive_emergency_count`, `alert_frequency_per_hour`, `burst_score`, `inter_alert_interval_mean`, `payload_entropy`, `regularity_coefficient`, `inter_interval_variance`, `topic_diversity`.
Imputation fixed a priori: interval mean NaN → 60 min, regularity NaN → 0, rolling std NaN → 0, others → 0.

Notes:
- **Rule revision 1:** redundancy is computed on alert windows (zero-inflation artifact otherwise); made before any attack/Zone B/C window was examined; the unrevised result is kept as `selected_features_rev0.json`.
- `consecutive_emergency_count` is the 0/1 alert flag: on all 647 Zone A alert windows it equals 1, so its correlation there is undefined and the redundancy rule cannot drop it. (Every such window has ≥ 5 emergencies.)
- `alert_frequency_per_hour` is the emergency count (windows are 60 min); `n_emergency` was dropped as an exact duplicate.
- `regularity_coefficient` and `inter_interval_variance` have Spearman 0.997 but Pearson 0.175 on alert windows; the conjunctive rule keeps both. They carry essentially one piece of information. Kept for this fit; the threshold is **not** revised again.
- `temporal_clustering_score` is nearly constant on normal traffic (1.3% of Zone A windows, ~6% of alert windows differ from the mode) and is dropped as degenerate. An earlier selection rule kept it because its selection scored class separation on the labeled file.
- `freq_rolling_std_5w` includes the current window, and `alert_rate_acceleration` compares the two halves of the same hour, so neither uses future data.
- **Disclosure:** the candidate pool was designed with A2/A5 in mind; label-free selection removes labels from the selection, not from the design.

## 4. Detector
- **Score = isolation-forest rank only.** `StandardScaler` + `IsolationForest(n_estimators=300)` fitted on the 323 Zone A fit hours. `n_estimators` was fixed a priori, not searched. The rank is taken against the 57 held-out Zone A hours. With one detector this is a monotone transform and doesn't change the p-values.
- **The LSTM forecaster is ablation-only:** too little contiguous normal history on the hourly grid (153 training and 24 held-out sequences; only 51 of 119 Zone B hours have 3 h of history). Its losses were 14–19% (training) and 4–9% (held-out) below predicting the training mean.
- **Conformal p-value:** p = (1 + #{Zone B scores ≥ s}) / (119 + 1); smallest attainable p = 0.0083. The threshold comes from the Zone B calibration, not from Zone A, because alert-hour rates differ (A 19%, B 12%).
- **Operating point α = 0.05** (alarm if p ≤ 0.05). α = 0.01 is reported as nominal only.
- **Seeds:** seed 0 is primary; seeds 1 and 2 are reported as the spread.

## 5. Evaluation and gate G2 (all on the hourly complete-hour grid)
- **False alarms:** on the 133 complete hours of the **uninjected** Zone C. The G2 rule is **≤ 1 false alarm** (two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound: 0 → 2.7%, 1 → 4.1%, 2 → 5.3%). α = 0.01 can't be confirmed (0 alarms bound the rate at 2.7%).
- **Detection:** on the injected copies; window-level TPR at α = 0.05 on complete hours that overlap an episode. Campaign recall = share of episodes with ≥ 1 alarm on an overlapping complete hour. **G2 copy = copy 0**; copies 1–2 are the placement spread. Recall is reported per copy with Clopper–Pearson intervals (n = 30), never pooled.
- **G2 passes if,** on seed 0, copy 0, for A5-S and A5-R: campaign recall ≥ 24/30, window-level TPR competitive with the best baseline , and ≤ 1 false alarm on clean Zone C. A5-M, A5-P and A5-U are reported, not gated.
- **Merged-rate caveats** (`episode_rates.csv`): A5-U is sub-threshold only as the injected process (merged 3.34/h, 49% of episodes > 2.47/h); A5-M and A5-R are alert-anchored replays (bursts), not rate-matched.

## 6. What is not allowed after the freeze
No change to features, imputation, detector, hours, α, seeds, copies or gate after Zone C is scored. Any later change is a new protocol version, reported as such, with the earlier results kept.

## Erratum (recorded after the freeze, before any Zone C score; procedure unchanged)
Section 4 says the rank normalization "is a monotone transform and doesn't change the p-values". That is wrong.
Ranking against only 57 held-out hours rounds the score into 58 levels and puts every hour more anomalous than all 57
into one top level. On Zone B, 96 of 119 hours share rank 39/57 and 4 sit at 57/57. So the smallest p a new hour can
get is 5/120 = 0.0417, not 0.0083. On seed 0, α = 0.05 fires only at rank 56/57 (p = 0.05) or 57/57 (p = 0.0417); on
seeds 1–2 the levels differ slightly, and on seed 1 only 57/57 fires. α = 0.01 cannot fire. The frozen procedure is
evaluated as is. The raw-score isolation forest is among the baselines, so the cost of this rounding is visible.

## Calibration amendment (written after Zone C was scored; original results kept)
**The original verdict stands as recorded: G2 failed** (seed 0, copy 0: A5-S 30/30, A5-R 29/30, 6 false alarms on 133 clean hours, rule ≤ 1).

**Why the rule changes.** "≤ 1 false alarm" required the 95% upper bound on the false-alarm rate to be ≤ 5%. A detector operating exactly at α = 0.05 expects 6.65 alarms on 133 hours and raises ≤ 1 with probability 0.9%. More test hours don't fix this: an interval around an observed 5% rate always extends above 5%. The rule tested "rate well below α", not calibration.

**Amended false-alarm criterion (the only change).** One-sided exact binomial test of H0: rate ≤ α = 0.05 on the clean Zone C hours, at level 0.05. On 133 hours it rejects at ≥ 12 alarms (P(X ≥ 12) = 0.035). Report the count, the Clopper–Pearson interval and the tail probability. Not rejecting shows the detector is not detectably miscalibrated. It does **not** show the rate is below 5%.
- BridgeGuard seed 0: 6/133, interval [0.017, 0.096], tail P(X ≥ 6) = 0.658, not rejected. **G2 under the amendment: pass.**
- No scorer in the table is rejected (largest count: CUSUM, 9/133, tail 0.222).

**Unoperationalized clause.** The original G2 also asked for window-level TPR "competitive with the best baseline". No number was fixed, and the original code did not evaluate it. The amendment doesn't invent a threshold after the fact. The clause is reported descriptively (seed 0, copy 0: A5-S 0.93 vs 1.00 for LOF/kNN; A5-R 0.82 vs 0.86 for OCSVM/LOF/kNN) and is not part of either verdict.

**Status of the Zone C table.** It's the **development-data study**. It already compared all scorers on attacks, so it isn't a clean test of whichever one did best. The frozen forest rank stays the development-data method. No detector is promoted from this table, and OCSVM, LOF and kNN aren't separated cleanly enough to pick one.

**Readings corrected from the same file** (`results/dev/eval_zoneC.json`, `episode_rates.csv`):
- A5-U, BridgeGuard seed 0, copy 0: the 6 missed episodes are the quietest (≤ 2.0 alerts/h). It detects 11/17 episodes at ≤ 2.47/h and 13/13 above.
- OCSVM, LOF and kNN are better on A5-U (29–30/30 on every copy). They don't win every cell: OCSVM detects 7/30 A5-M episodes against BridgeGuard's 12/30, and LOF detects 28/30 A5-R against 29/30.
- The raw isolation forest is a separate fit with the library default of 100 trees. It doesn't isolate the rank-rounding cost. Its A5-U recall is 20–25/30 across seeds and copies. The 14/30 is the ranked forest on seed 1. OCSVM, LOF, kNN, ECOD, the rate rule and CUSUM give the same result for every seed.
- The calibrated rate rule alarms only at ≥ 15 alerts in an hour (Zone B 95th percentile: 13.1). Most A5-S/R/P episodes stay under that, even though their average rate is above 2.47/h. That's why the rule misses them.
- A5-M is the main limitation: 7–16/30 episodes for the distance methods across copies, hour-level detection 0.09–0.26.
- LSTM-AE and KitNET were not run; reasons are in `baselines.NOT_RUN`.

**Testbed rule.** Testbed Zone B has no attacks, so it can't choose a scorer. Before any testbed Zone C score, write down the full scorer list and the primary scorer in a frozen document, then score Zone C once.

**Corrections to the amended readings.**
- **Rate rule:** on copy 0, 5/30 A5-S, 10/30 A5-R and 4/30 A5-P episodes contain an hour of ≥ 15 alerts. Those are the episodes the rate rule detects. (The earlier 3–20% figure counted episode *average* rates, which is the wrong measure.)
- **A2 spillover:** BridgeGuard, seed 0, A2 copy 0, alarms on 14 of the 57 outside-episode hours.
  - Twelve start 1–39 min after an episode ends. In each, `freq_rolling_std_5w` is the only feature that differs from the clean hour.
  - The other two (213 and 378 min later) also alarm on the clean file (p 0.042) and on the mimic copy.
  - In the 378-min hour the rolling std is 0.00044 instead of 0. This is floating-point residue in pandas' rolling variance after the A2 flood, and it doesn't change the alarm.
  - The clean false-alarm count stays 6/133. Per-hour rates on attacked streams must separate the following hour (fixed in `TESTBED_SCORERS.md`).
- **LSTM-AE's 0/133 is conservative, not calibrated.** Its 47 unscored Zone B hours sit at −∞, so a scored hour alarms only if it ranks in the top 5 of the 72 scored Zone B hours: for a new hour exchangeable with those 72, that has probability 6/73 ≈ 8.2%, so about 7.3 alarms are expected on the 89 scored clean hours; even at 5/72 the chance of none is (1 − 5/72)^89 = 0.0017. Adjacent scored hours share two of their three input hours, so this is a size estimate, not a formal p-value. The amended test doesn't reject 0/133 because it counts the unscored hours. Its scored Zone C hours sit lower than Zone B's (a Zone B → C shift for this scorer). Coverage explains its A2 misses (22 of 22 scorable episodes detected) and most of A5-S (26 of 27). It doesn't explain A5-R (19 of 22 scorable) or A5-U (11 of 19 scorable).
